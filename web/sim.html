Simulation

The solar system, SPHEREx's orbit around the Earth, a body's track on the sky and its light, all on one clock. Positions come from Kepler's equation, speeds from Newton, light from Planck's law. SPHEREx's orbit and its rule for where it looks were measured from real exposures. The end of the page says what each calculation was checked against.

04

Light

The flux reaching SPHEREx from the body over 0.75–5 µm. It has two sources: reflected sunlight and the body's own heat. Both the Sun and the body are treated as blackbodies.

Dashed: reflected · dotted: thermal · solid: total · flat line: measured limit · squares: the flux SPHEREx measured for WISE 0855.

05

SPHEREx's orbit

The spacecraft passes over the Earth's poles, and its orbital plane turns once a year so it stays in the same place relative to the Sun. Height, inclination and node were measured from the spacecraft positions of 8989 cached exposures. The amber dot is the model; the hollow rings are the spacecraft's real positions on those days.

Drag: turn. Set the clock to "1 s = 1 min" for this panel. The lit half faces the Sun.

  • Planet positions come from mean orbits. These include the planets' pull on each other on average, but leave errors of up to 10′ for Jupiter and Saturn. The table is valid only for 1800–2050.
  • Planet X moves under the Sun's pull alone. Its pull on the planets, and theirs on it, is not included.
  • Light is computed as a blackbody. For real cold atmospheres this can be badly wrong: it underpredicts WISE 0855's 4.4–5 µm flux by a factor of about 19 (measured).
  • SPHEREx's pointing rule (80–120° from the Sun) says when a field can be seen. It is not the spacecraft's real observing schedule.
  • No Moon, no Earth rotation, no atmosphere. The clock is UTC; the 69 s difference from TT is ignored.
